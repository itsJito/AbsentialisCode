# ABSENTIALIS — Revised Canon Foundation

Status: agreed narrative foundation, 12 October 2026. Designer-facing; contains major spoilers.

This revision replaces the previous narrative framework. It records accepted foundations and explicitly separates unresolved design questions. It is not a completed campaign script or a claim that proposed systems are already implemented.

## 1. Core premise

Absentialis was once a city worth loving. People built homes, friendships, families, routines, and futures there. A real, Chernobyl-like disaster at its power plant devastated the city. Quarantine, displacement, loss, contamination, and the breakdown of ordinary life transformed it into a nightmare of despair.

The disaster was real. The city is real. Its surviving inhabitants are real. The player's actions have lasting consequences for them.

The protagonist died in that original disaster. They have nevertheless returned, carrying an unfinished promise to someone they are now searching for. They initially believe they survived. When they die during an operation, they wake again at their hideout and initially interpret the experience as a bad dream. The world has not reset.

The central promise is: **“I said I'd come back.”**

The story begins with finding someone. It develops into a confrontation with what the promise meant, what that person wants now, and whether the protagonist can accept an outcome different from the rescue they imagined.

## 2. What replaces the old lore

PROJECT VEIL is removed entirely. There is no replacement secret project responsible for the catastrophe, resurrection, or the city's underlying horror.

The catastrophe is an actual power-plant disaster, not a cover story for a covert experiment or weapon. Its precise causes, institutional failures, and sequence remain to be written. Human wrongdoing may exist within that history, but a hidden experiment is not the master explanation.

The Chernobyl-like reference establishes the character of the disaster and its human aftermath. Supernatural returns and fictional creatures are not presented as real-world radiation effects. The power plant is not treated as an ordinary reactor that detonates like a nuclear weapon.

The old mandatory city-destroying finale is no longer canon. The city's eventual fate belongs to the new branching campaign and remains to be designed. The former nine-year timeline, five mandatory opening investigations, and old explanations of creatures are not automatically carried forward.

Existing names remain reserved and unchanged. Where a name's former explanation depended on discarded lore, its new role is unresolved; it must not silently restore that lore. PROJECT VEIL is the explicit removal authorized for this rewrite.

## 3. The city before and after

The emotional foundation is the destruction of a loved home. Pre-disaster Absentialis need not have been perfect, but its warmth was genuine. Its residents had lives beyond becoming victims, enemies, or clues.

Environmental storytelling should show what people valued: shared meals, repaired belongings, familiar shops, family photographs, small celebrations, and everyday acts of care. These details must fit existing locations rather than require changes to the map.

Some care survives in the present. People still protect neighbors, share food, maintain shelters, and form attachments. Those moments make loss meaningful and give faction decisions something worth protecting.

Horror grows from the collision between ordinary human attachment and a place where familiar things no longer behave reliably. Quiet, anticipation, intimate recognition, and uncertainty matter as much as violence. Action remains physical and dangerous; it does not replace the personal mystery.

## 4. The protagonist and the missing person

The player creates their character. The story does not require a military, scientific, or plant-worker occupation. Death occurred as a consequence of the original disaster; the exact place and circumstances remain open so backgrounds can remain varied.

The protagonist remembers enough to pursue someone but does not initially understand their own death and return. Fragmented recollection is not permission to invent an unrelated secret personality or overwrite the identity the player established.

The missing person is real and has an independent life, history, loyalties, and wishes. They are not merely a reward for completing the campaign. Their relationship to the protagonist is selected or approved by the player. Their identity may be generated, but their emotional history is authored coherently.

The search may reveal that this person does not want to be found, rescued, or taken away. Whether that is true in a particular campaign belongs to its authored history. The game must not force every player character into being abusive, possessive, or secretly murderous.

The defining tension is that love, responsibility, guilt, and the need to repair the past can pull in different directions. Finding someone does not give the protagonist ownership of their future.

## 5. Personalization and replayability

The accepted direction is a civilian missing-person inquiry form integrated naturally into the opening. It requires no particular occupation. Its exact presentation and fields remain subject to implementation.

Player inputs establish the relationship, a generated or chosen identity, an identifying keepsake, and the protagonist's account of their separation or promise. These are believable personal details, not a psychological diagnosis questionnaire.

The game's underlying history is chosen and locked at campaign/profile creation. It persists across deployments, deaths, and faction changes. Evidence cannot change the historical answer merely because the player has joined another faction.

Three authored foundations guide development:

| Foundation | Starting belief | Central challenge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Promise | “I said I'd come back. I have to bring them out.” | They may have built a life or chosen a future that the rescue would destroy. |
| The Debt | “They saved me. I owe them.” | Repayment or forgiveness may be refused; saving the protagonist may have harmed someone else. |
| The Secret | “I kept quiet to protect them.” | They may have needed the protagonist to speak and may now want the truth exposed. |

**The Promise is the primary development version.** The Debt and The Secret are accepted directions for later variants, not three completed campaigns or an obligation to ship all three immediately.

Personalization should come from compatible authored histories, faction involvement, clue ownership, and consequential player behavior. Random names and locations alone do not create a personal story. Establish compatibility rules before expanding combinations.

The player's choices shape their response to the truth and the world's future. They do not retroactively manufacture a different past.

## 6. Death and return

The player died in the original disaster and has physically consequential experiences in the present. Calling a failed operation a nightmare is their initial interpretation, not the final explanation of the game world.

Rules of return:

1. Death does not rewind the world. Time passes and established consequences persist.
2. Equipment loss and extraction stakes remain meaningful, subject to the existing Pick 3 protection system.
3. Other people's deaths remain consequential. The protagonist cannot assume companions or enemies will return.
4. The player returns to the persistent hideout. That location must eventually have a meaningful connection to their life, death, or promise; the precise connection is unresolved.
5. Return restores the ability to act, not everything lost in the failed operation.
6. The condition is unusual and important without making the protagonist a prophesied savior.
7. The protagonist's original death is intended as a substantial midgame revelation, leaving time to act on it rather than reducing it to a last-minute twist.

Corpse behavior, return timing, NPC witnessing, and the exact nature of the returning body require consistent internal rules before implementation. No particular answer is canonized yet.

No additional mandatory permanent punishment for each death is established. The existing survival economy supplies stakes; revelation pacing should not depend on forcing repeated deaths.

## 7. The supernatural

Something genuinely impossible exists in Absentialis. The city is not wholly a dream, private hallucination, or symbolic population created for the player.

The supernatural connects to attachment, loss, memory, denial, and unfinished intentions. Its complete mechanism is not yet fixed. The earlier idea that unacknowledged things can acquire physical presence remains a working motif, not a universal law already approved for every encounter.

Authors must know enough rules to keep observations consistent. Players may remain uncertain about the ultimate explanation. Concrete facts about human actions should be discoverable even when uncanny experiences remain unresolved.

The city is not a fair moral judge. Shame is not proof of guilt; lack of remorse is not innocence. Horror must not imply that victims deserved the disaster or that accepting grief automatically entitles someone to escape.

Later scenes may respond to clearly framed player choices. They should not diagnose the player from ordinary tactical retreat or treat every failed rescue as a moral failure.

## 8. Factions and relationships

All existing factions, bases, and character names remain unchanged. Membership, betrayal, squads, and consequential alliances remain central. Faction members have independent material concerns and internal disagreements.

| Existing faction | Personal temptation | Stakes once the player's condition becomes credible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mercenaries — Halberd Group | A debt can be priced and settled. | Employers see a valuable discovery; individuals may offer genuine protection through compromised contracts. |
| Military — 3rd Cordon Battalion | Duty can explain what could not be saved. | The returning protagonist creates a containment dilemma; officers differ over protection, confinement, and escape. |
| Loners — Saint Agnes Shelter / Residential Families | Making yourself useful can repair something. | Compassion competes with limited resources and grief over why others receive no second chance. |
| Bandits — Kerosene Boys / Rail Yard Crew | Nobody owns your future. | Freedom from institutional custody may come with pressure to accept otherwise suicidal work. |

These are narrative pressures, not uniform personalities or predetermined moral rankings. The person most sympathetic to the protagonist can still exploit their apparent expendability.

Faction knowledge of resurrection must spread through credible witnesses and evidence rather than automatic global awareness. Political conflicts should become inseparable from the personal search without making every faction exist solely to serve it.

## 9. Campaign and mission structure

The team's mission framework remains the design backbone:

**Personal motivation → investigative lead → dangerous expedition → environmental discovery → escalating opposition → moral dilemma → consequential decision → unexpected revelation → shifting allegiances → greater mystery → next mission.**

Individual missions contain Hook, Objective, Journey, Evidence, Complication, Decision, Resolution, and Knowledge Shift. Beats may overlap or unfold across return visits; they are not eight mandatory cutscenes.

| Act | Narrative purpose |
| --- | --- |
| I — Personal Stakes | Survive, establish the promise, and begin searching. |
| II — Expanding Conspiracy | Discover competing accounts, faction interests, and the missing person's independent life. Concealment concerns human choices and the disaster's aftermath, not a secret project behind everything. |
| III — Systemic Revelation | Confront disaster records, the protagonist's original death, and the ways institutions and communities responded. Hidden experiments are removed from the inherited act formula. |
| IV — Escalating Conflict | The search and faction commitments create irreversible costs, competing obligations, and changing alliances. |
| V — Truth and Recontextualization | Understand the separation, the promise, and the missing person's wishes; confront the protagonist's interpretation of them. |
| VI — Consequential Resolution | Make final commitments that determine personal and world outcomes. Exact endings remain unwritten. |

Every narrative mission answers a question and introduces a consequential one. Each objective has an intelligible immediate purpose and a deeper significance. Knowledge shifts may be quiet confirmations rather than twists. Betrayal and spectacle must not become compulsory mission punctuation.

Use a small number of major commitments with visible effects on relationships, objectives, resources, access, and outcomes. Branches can converge while preserving the consequences of earlier decisions. No final choice should erase the campaign's history.

Missions occur within the existing deployment/extraction loop. They support retreat, partial discovery, revisiting, multiple approaches, and unpredictable encounters. Critical knowledge cannot depend entirely on one vulnerable NPC or irreplaceable object. Persistent discoveries survive player death.

## 10. Gameplay and asset boundaries

Preserve the existing map and district names: Downtown, Residential, Industrial, Munincipal, and Barrens. Do not rename locations or invent required new map geometry.

Preserve existing faction, character, enemy, equipment, and item names. Narrative reinterpretation does not authorize changing weapon statistics, progression, economy, or established mechanics.

The single-player voxel extraction survival shooter structure remains: persistent hideout, squads, contested deployments, limited inventory, scavenging, objectives, and extraction. Backpack progression reaches its maximum at rank 20; prestige is available at rank 50. Pick 3 protects three eligible items after death, including jewelry.

An existing watch, ring, medal, or set of dog tags can serve as the personal keepsake. The accepted design direction lets the player choose whether to spend a Pick 3 slot protecting it. Keeping, losing, or releasing it is not automatically a moral score. Losing it must not softlock the story. Specific item selection and implementation remain open.

Prestige does not yet have a canonical supernatural explanation. Secret accessories remain gameplay rewards rather than evidence explaining resurrection. No new hidden experiments are required to account for existing enemy designs; their revised origins remain open.

## 11. Decisions still to make

- Exact disaster date, chronology, failures, evacuation history, and the interval before the player's return.
- Why this protagonist returns, the limits of the phenomenon, corpse behavior, and the hideout's connection.
- Missing-person form fields, compatible relationships and histories, and the missing person's present circumstances.
- The role of VESPER and other retained names formerly attached to discarded explanations.
- Revised origins and narrative roles of infected and named threats.
- Major faction commitments, concrete missions, and divergent endings.
- Post-ending play and prestige continuity.

Do not promote examples from brainstorming into settled facts without a further decision.

## Narrative north star

**You died promising to return to someone. Now you keep returning. The city and its people must live with what you do each time you wake—and finding the person you love may mean accepting a future you cannot choose for them.**
