# ABSENTIALIS
## Faction Missions — 20-Mission Rewrite

**Design draft • 12 October 2026 • Canon foundation applies**

Four independent chains. Five missions each. Fixed sequence; variable circumstances and lasting consequences. These arcs introduce the city and feed the personal campaign; they do not prescribe its ending.

### Shared rules

- **Accept in the world:** Every mission is personally offered by its faction advisor during deployment, including missions 2–5. Rumors and environmental clues lead players to them; no automatic acceptance or hideout quest board.
- **Linear progression:** Resolve 1 to unlock 2, through 5. Aid, betrayal, and documented failure count as distinct resolutions. Leaving mid-task suspends it. No other faction chain is a prerequisite; chains may be interleaved.
- **Persistent advisors:** Four new, fixed nonleader NPCs advise whichever leader is generated. Proposed implementation: protected, noncombat contacts at existing safe interaction points; relocate between deployments if control changes. Discoverable notices identify their new location. They do not die and resurrect.
- **Consequential hostility:** Betrayal can turn subsequent work into restitution or a hostile investigation. Advisors offer those variants under a localized parley; faction patrols remain hostile. Advancing the chain does not restore friendship or guarantee faction rewards.
- **Dynamic content:** Lock history and personal clues at profile creation. Select supported locations and participants when accepting a mission; persist them thereafter. Patrols, survivors, ownership, and prior choices change execution, never the historical answer.
- **Completion:** Finish the objective or establish its failure, then report to the advisor in person during deployment. Recoveries require physical handoff; observations survive death. Lost cargo requires recovery or a reduced-outcome settlement, never free duplication. Escort failure leads to accounting for the loss, not a replacement person.
- **Resilient evidence:** Each essential fact has a physical source and an independent witness or record. Dead witnesses leave consequences; alternate evidence keeps the chain possible. No mandatory boss kills or new map geometry.
- **Rewards:** One-time story XP for resolution. Successful contracted work adds the faction's usual cash or supplies; access benefits below depend on the decision. Values remain with balancing.

### The advisors

| Faction / district | New trusted advisor | Role and first deployment encounter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mercenaries — Halberd Group / Downtown | **Nika Beridze** | Contract assessor; precise, dry, ashamed of treating grief as a claim. Found checking abandoned property claims at the police HQ. |
| Military — 3rd Cordon Battalion / Munincipal | **Lt. Vera Sokolova** | Civilian liaison; values discipline because she has seen panic kill. Found reconciling civilian passage requests at the base. |
| Loners — Saint Agnes Shelter / Residential Families / Residential | **Kateryna Melnyk** | Shelter coordinator; remembers everyone’s preferences, struggles to accept refusal. Found packing household deliveries at the shelter. |
| Bandits — Kerosene Boys / Rail Yard Crew / Barrens | **Wade Ellis** | Route broker; dependable about passage, evasive about who pays its price. Found settling transport debts by the freight track. |

**Card key:** Assignment = hook, objective, journey. Discovery = evidence and knowledge shift. Decision = dilemma and persistent resolution. Variation = complication and response to world state. Each closing lead explains the next mission.

## MERCENARIES — What Can Be Owned?

**Perspective:** Downtown survived through trade and protection. Halberd believes enforceable agreements keep it functioning; its contracts can also turn somebody’s home into somebody else’s asset.

### 1. Property of the Absent

- **Assignment:** Nika offers access to tracing records for recovering a sealed package from a Downtown apartment through occupied streets.
- **Discovery:** The package contains household valuables, entrusted for safekeeping during evacuation. A meal remains laid out beside the packing boxes. Its contents were later sold as abandoned property.
- **Decision:** Honor the buyer’s claim for cash and Halberd trust, or return it to a verified surviving owner for civilian cooperation and a disputed contract.
- **Variation:** The owner is present or has relocated to another existing apartment; current occupiers determine negotiation or infiltration. The custody receipt identifies the grocery in mission 2.

### 2. Paid in Advance

- **Assignment:** Trace the receipt and escort a supply carrier from the Downtown grocery to police HQ; payment keeps a protection contract alive.
- **Discovery:** Residents pooled valuables for deliveries. Their old ledger shows a neighborhood feeding itself before charity became a bill.
- **Decision:** Deliver the contracted stock for better faction supplies, or divert it to the contributing households for improved civilian barter and a contract penalty.
- **Variation:** Mission 1 decides whether residents warn of danger or conceal the carrier. Patrol pressure permits stealth, bargaining, or combat. Delivery accounts expose unpaid escorts, leading to mission 3.

### 3. Outstanding Wages

- **Assignment:** Nika wants a missing escort brought back from a Downtown apartment before the employer declares desertion.
- **Discovery:** The escort stayed to guard residents after wages stopped. A casualty report blames them for losses incurred before their deployment. Nika previously accepted that report without checking.
- **Decision:** Return them to service for an escort ally and faction favor, or obtain their release by presenting the pay records, improving civilian trust but losing contracted support.
- **Variation:** Earlier deliveries determine residents’ willingness to testify. If the escort died, recover their account and decide compensation instead. The disputed report names an Industrial recovery job.

### 4. The Cost of Silence

- **Assignment:** Recover that job’s data drive from existing plant offices in Industrial before another recovery team removes it.
- **Discovery:** It documents post-disaster claims denied to workers’ families—not a hidden cause of the accident. A disconnected office phone rings as the player reads a message promising someone would return.
- **Decision:** Hand over the original for a substantial payment and restricted access, or distribute verified copies to claimants, losing the client’s backing and enabling restitution demands.
- **Variation:** The escort or their surviving contacts provide access if helped; otherwise negotiate or infiltrate. Prior evidence determines which denials can be proved. The resulting dispute triggers mission 5.

### 5. No Further Obligation

- **Assignment:** At the grocery, oversee settlement of the disputed protection contract: escort residents to the meeting, verify goods, and secure the exchange.
- **Discovery:** The buyer will continue supplies only if residents surrender salvage rights. Nika admits contracts can preserve a neighborhood while gradually taking it away.
- **Decision:** Enforce exclusivity for reliable Halberd supply and stronger patrols, or back residents’ refusal for independent trade with weaker protection. Evidence from mission 4 enables a narrower settlement with reduced rewards on both sides.
- **Variation:** Earlier residents and escorts attend, refuse, or are absent; substitutes represent surviving households. Outcome persists as Downtown trade policy. The tracing archive now provides a canon-compatible lead in the personal search.

## MILITARY — Who Gets Through?

**Perspective:** Munincipal remembers the disaster as a sequence of impossible orders. The battalion keeps danger contained while deciding whose freedom can be restricted in the name of everybody else.

### 1. Remain Where You Are

- **Assignment:** Vera asks you to verify a distress call near the airport and locate the overdue patrol sent to investigate.
- **Discovery:** An old evacuation recording repeats instructions to wait. Fresh civilian footprints show that people still mistake it for a current announcement.
- **Decision:** Disable the misleading broadcast, reducing civilian exposure but losing a usable signal landmark; or correct it and preserve the beacon, accepting increased traffic to an exposed location.
- **Variation:** The patrol is pinned down or has withdrawn, leaving its field report. Infected movement changes the approach. Recent passage slips lead to mission 2.

### 2. Names at the Gate

- **Assignment:** Escort permit applicants from administration to the base while verifying the slips recovered at the airport.
- **Discovery:** One household’s valid permit excludes its dependent. The soldiers’ register records previous violence caused by unidentified arrivals; the restriction has a real rationale.
- **Decision:** Enforce the permit, separating the group and gaining official trust, or vouch for the dependent, preserving the household but accepting tighter searches and responsibility for their passage.
- **Variation:** Mission 1 determines crowd size and available witnesses. A dead applicant leaves documents and surviving dependents, not a new identical family. A guard asks you to investigate his overdue relief patrol.

### 3. Orders After Dark

- **Assignment:** Locate the relief patrol around the airport and establish why it abandoned its position.
- **Discovery:** Its members are recovering belongings from the original evacuation. Their former comrades died waiting for relief; the current patrol keeps trying to finish that old duty. An empty radio channel counts one more soldier than is present.
- **Decision:** Escort them back for disciplinary review and restored patrol coverage, or cover their absence while they finish, preserving their trust but leaving a route less protected.
- **Variation:** Prior permit treatment determines civilian help. Living soldiers testify; their report supplies the lead if they died. They identify conflicting evacuation orders in administration.

### 4. The Order That Arrived Late

- **Assignment:** Compare administration’s evacuation orders with dispatch timestamps at the airport through active patrol territory.
- **Discovery:** A genuine warning reached an official who delayed forwarding it to avoid panic. Later responders were blamed for acting late. The records explain a preventable loss within the real disaster.
- **Decision:** Submit proof through command for quiet correction and continued access, or circulate authenticated copies to survivors, improving civilian cooperation while hardliners tighten checkpoint scrutiny.
- **Variation:** A cooperative patrol helps verify signatures; an archive duplicate replaces missing testimony. If the central campaign has already confirmed your death, Vera privately recognizes the matching casualty entry. A present-day passage dispute tests the precedent in mission 5.

### 5. The Line We Hold

- **Assignment:** Secure one existing route between administration and the base so displaced civilians can reach shelter inside the cordon.
- **Discovery:** Closing the route protects a depleted checkpoint but strands people in exposed buildings. Opening it requires troops to surrender control of another approach.
- **Decision:** Back strict passage checks for stronger containment and fewer admissions, or support supervised humanitarian passage for safer civilian movement and thinner perimeter patrols.
- **Variation:** Saved personnel and earlier permit records determine escort strength and verification options. Hostile players negotiate through Vera’s parley rather than commanding troops. The route policy persists; a verified evacuation contact becomes a lead for your search.

## LONERS — When Does Care Become Control?

**Perspective:** Residential was a place of ordinary affection. Its survivors preserve home through care, but they disagree about whether keeping someone safe gives them the right to decide for that person.

### 1. A Place at the Table

- **Assignment:** Kateryna offers help with your inquiry if you check a household that stopped collecting food and deliver its ration through Residential.
- **Discovery:** One resident still sets two places. A shared recipe and years of repaired crockery establish the relationship before the empty chair becomes unsettling.
- **Decision:** Support their choice to remain, committing shelter supplies to deliveries, or persuade them to relocate, consolidating care but leaving their home empty. Refusal remains possible.
- **Variation:** Choose a supported house; the resident’s mobility changes the delivery or escort task. Their account, or an old care note, points to medicine stored in the mansion.

### 2. Enough for One More

- **Assignment:** Recover usable medical supplies from the mansion for a shelter shortage; navigate occupied rooms and unreliable sightlines.
- **Discovery:** Someone maintained a neighborhood medicine cupboard after official services collapsed. Its remaining stock is also sustaining a concealed patient.
- **Decision:** Take the stock to improve shelter treatment, or leave the patient’s share and return with less, preserving a vulnerable household at a measurable communal cost.
- **Variation:** Mission 1’s resident can introduce the caretaker or leave a written referral. Survival determines who can explain the cupboard. A treatment note identifies a volunteer who never returned.

### 3. The Room with the Light On

- **Assignment:** Find the volunteer in an existing house and escort them home if they consent.
- **Discovery:** They have been tending an empty bedroom, convinced someone inside needs them. Through the door, the player hears the missing person speak a phrase selected from their established history. Physical clues do not prove who spoke.
- **Decision:** Persuade the volunteer to return for improved shelter staffing, or support limited visits, preserving trust but consuming supplies. If persuasion fails, accept refusal rather than force an escort.
- **Variation:** Prior medical allocation changes confidence in Kateryna. If the volunteer died, recover their final instructions and decide whether to continue deliveries. Their notes reveal letters Kateryna withheld.

### 4. Letters Never Delivered

- **Assignment:** Retrieve the correspondence from its established shelter storage and verify one recipient at a Residential house before distribution.
- **Discovery:** Kateryna withheld departures and bad news, fearing residents would leave or give up. Some protection depended on denying people information about their own lives.
- **Decision:** Deliver the verified letters for autonomy and greater trust in you, accepting departures and reduced shelter labor; or entrust phased delivery to Kateryna, preserving staffing while extending her control.
- **Variation:** Earlier residents become recipients or leave relatives to receive their mail. Profile-consistent wording can echo your promise without inventing a new past. One recipient requests help leaving, creating mission 5.

### 5. Leave the Light On

- **Assignment:** Help the household from mission 4 choose between remaining at the shelter and relocating to an existing Residential home; deliver supplies or escort them through danger.
- **Discovery:** Staying offers practical safety, but the resident wants a life beyond being cared for. Kateryna fears that accepting departure means failing them.
- **Decision:** Back voluntary independent living for a new civilian supply contact and reduced shelter reserves, or secure an agreed stay for stronger communal resources. Never force either choice; failed persuasion changes the work to supporting their decision.
- **Variation:** Prior letters, medicine, and survivors determine what support is available. A known contact agrees to pass your inquiry onward; finding your missing person remains a separate, unresolved question.

## BANDITS — What Is the Price of Freedom?

**Perspective:** Barrens remembers escape routes when official doors closed. The crews inherited both a tradition of getting people out and the temptation to charge desperate people whatever they can take.

### 1. Passage Owed

- **Assignment:** Wade offers a route contact in exchange for recovering a payment ledger from a stranded courier near the freight track.
- **Discovery:** Beside current debts are original evacuation entries marked paid with watches, food, or nothing. Some people were helped because they were neighbors.
- **Decision:** Collect the surviving courier’s debt for crew favor and cash, or waive it for a dependable local guide and a smaller payout.
- **Variation:** The courier is hiding or injured; if dead, settle the claim against their recorded guarantor instead. Their cargo docket points to a disputed farm delivery.

### 2. Protection Money

- **Assignment:** Accompany a collector to the farm and establish why food payments stopped; reach the site through contested Barrens routes.
- **Discovery:** The farm’s promised guards never arrived. Its residents paid for protection that existed only in the ledger.
- **Decision:** Enforce collection for stronger crew supply and alienated farmers, or redirect the payment into an actual guard shift, losing your commission and improving farm cooperation.
- **Variation:** Mission 1’s guide can reveal a quieter approach. Current occupants determine dialogue or recovery of accounts. The missing guards left to intercept a jail release, leading to mission 3.

### 3. A Door Left Open

- **Assignment:** Locate that interception near the jail and recover a guide accused of selling crew routes.
- **Discovery:** The guide disclosed a path to reunite a separated household. The path is now compromised; saving one family exposed other travelers.
- **Decision:** Return the guide for a negotiated debt settlement, preserving crew control but restricting their freedom, or help them leave, gaining an independent contact and losing route privileges.
- **Variation:** Previous collection choices determine whether farmers shelter or expose the guide. If the guide died, negotiate over the household’s debt using their notes. Their account identifies who purchased the route information.

### 4. The Price of a Name

- **Assignment:** Wade sends you to a freight-track exchange to intercept a ledger naming travelers and their destinations.
- **Discovery:** Wade sold access to cover crew losses, telling himself the buyer wanted tolls. The buyer now uses family connections to pressure travelers. A wind-up alarm clock rings from a packed bag whose owner is confirmed dead.
- **Decision:** Sell the ledger for a large payout and buyer access, or return sensitive pages to their owners for safer civilian passage, forfeiting the payment and exposing Wade’s deal.
- **Variation:** The guide’s fate changes access to the exchange; existing reputation selects negotiation, theft, or interception. An existing patrol or rival crew can interrupt. The buyer moves to enforce its claimed toll in mission 5.

### 5. Nobody Owns the Road

- **Assignment:** At an existing freight-track passage, escort a civilian shipment and settle the buyer’s toll claim before the crews retaliate.
- **Discovery:** Wade can preserve a dependable guarded route through exclusive fees, or break the monopoly without enough people to guarantee everyone’s safety.
- **Decision:** Uphold exclusive passage for predictable crew protection and charges, or open access for cheaper civilian movement and less reliable escorts. Farm cooperation can fund limited shared protection, at a continuing food cost.
- **Variation:** Earlier guides, farmers, and ledger recipients determine support and who arrives to oppose you. The route policy persists. A surviving travel contact—or their retained correspondence—provides a grounded lead in your personal search.

## Keeping the Twenty Missions Coherent

| Persistent decision | Visible result in later deployments |
| --- | --- |
| Downtown protection settlement | Patrol presence, civilian barter, and Halberd supply access. |
| Munincipal passage policy | Checkpoint scrutiny, civilian movement, and escort availability. |
| Residential care decisions | Which homes remain occupied, shelter stock, volunteer staffing, and civilian contacts. |
| Barrens toll settlement | Passage fees, guard reliability, and farm supply relationships. |

**Cross-faction reactions:** Public evidence or a witnessed action can change another faction’s attitude, prices, or assistance. No faction learns private decisions automatically. Policies govern different local resources, so completing one chain never erases another’s settlement.

**Personal mystery:** All four advisors can move the inquiry forward through their own contacts. Resolve clue ownership once per profile; later chains corroborate known facts or provide the next compatible lead. Do not give four contradictory last sightings, require all twenty missions, or reveal resurrection merely because one faction reached mission 4. These introduction arcs support the central campaign’s separate, corroborated midgame revelation.

**Production limit:** Author one base version and at most two situational variants per mission. Reuse existing interiors, items, reputation, patrol, trade, and squad systems. Required new support is advisor persistence/parley, mission-state variants, consent-aware escorts, and durable outcome flags; no new district, faction, boss, or supernatural rule is required.
