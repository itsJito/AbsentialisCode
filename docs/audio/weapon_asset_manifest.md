### What the firearm library is, and is not

Its master sheet (`Prepared Master Sheet.csv`, shipped with the library) describes every file. Each firearm has a
"near distance, front of shooter, left/right stereo" recording and a "mid distance, front of shooter, forward/back
stereo" recording, each holding two or more single shots; the three automatic weapons also have burst recordings.

- Perspectives available: **near** and **mid** only. There is no separate mechanical-only, interior, far-field or
  suppressed recording of any weapon.
- The near recordings reach digital full scale on most shots (see "flat-top samples" below): the muzzle blast
  overloaded the converter for a fraction of a millisecond. Nothing in the pipeline hides this; it is what a
  close microphone does, and the first millisecond of a gunshot is where it is least audible.
- Burst recordings are **not** used. Every round the game fires is assembled from single-shot recordings and
  scheduled by the engine, so automatic fire never plays a baked loop.

## Layers produced for each set (`assets/audio/weapons/<set>/`)

| Layer | File pattern | Format | How it is made |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transient | `<set>_tr<n>.wav` | 44.1 kHz 16-bit mono | the take above the 1100 Hz crossover, first 60 ms whole, cosine fade to 100 ms; begins 1 ms before the onset, no fade-in |
| Body | `<set>_bd<n>.wav` | 11.025 kHz 16-bit mono | the take below the crossover (zero-phase low-pass), up to 2.6 s |
| Tail | `<set>_tl<n>.wav` | 44.1 kHz 16-bit mono | what is left above the crossover after the transient window, up to 2.6 s |
| Distant | `<set>_ds<n>.wav` | 11.025 kHz 16-bit mono | the mid-distance take, low-passed at 2.2 kHz |

Transient + body + tail of one take sum back to that take (the split is complementary), so layering adds nothing
that was not recorded and removes nothing that was. Processing applied: mono fold-down, peak alignment to 0.9 within
a take, the crossover, cosine fades at the cut points, windowed-sinc sample-rate conversion, 1-LSB triangular dither.
No pitch shifting, compression, EQ or reverb is baked into any file.

## Gaps, substitutions and open items

- **Suppressed fire** has no recordings. It is rendered from the unsuppressed takes (transient and body reduced,
  low-passed, slower, far less reverb, action noise raised - `suppressed` in `content/weapon_audio.json`). This is a
  rendering, not a recording, and is the weakest part of the set.
- **Semi-automatic shotguns** (Riot, Striker) use pump-action 12-gauge recordings; the report is right for the
  cartridge, the action noise after it is assembled from generic handling sounds.
- **Magnum handguns** (Desert Eagle, Killer9, Handcannon) have no magnum-pistol recording in the library and use
  rifle-calibre recordings (.32 Winchester Special, .30-30, .30-06) played marginally faster or slower. They are loud
  and distinct from each other, but they are substitutes.
- **Punisher** uses a .22 pistol recording lowered in rate, **Matilda** a .380, **Tommy** a PPSh: substitutes of the
  right class.
- **Mechanical layers** are not recordings of the same firearms: the handling sources are generic (one of them
  recorded with airsoft replicas, as its page states). They are mixed low for that reason.
- **Takes per weapon**: most firearms have two near single shots in the library. The engine pairs the high band of
  one take with the low band of another and varies rate and level per shot, but two takes is two takes.
- **Asphalt and dirt footsteps** are derived from the concrete and grass recordings (table above).
- **Attribution to confirm**: the author credit for "Gun Reload Sounds" was not written down when the files were
  fetched. The page URL is above; the credit should be copied from it into `ATTRIBUTION.md` before any public release.
- Not used from the library: Arisaka, Mossberg (bolt-action 12 gauge), Ruger Single Six, Savage 10, SKS, Tikka and all
  burst files. They remain in `tools/audio/originals/` for future weapons.
